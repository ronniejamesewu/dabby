- **Dabby as open-source companion spec** — Two related but distinct artifacts: (1) **Open-source the full project** (generator, data model, website) with personal run data stripped — standard open-source playbook, well-understood, do it when the project feels stable enough. (2) **A portable companion spec** — the more interesting and harder thing, raised Session 94 while high and thinking clearly about it. The value isn't the logging tool; it's the *companion experience*: Claude coached to be genuinely useful in this domain, opinionated, epistemically honest, with a point of view on what the signal means and what it doesn't. That lives in the instructions layer (CLAUDE.md, Handoff_Notes, Handoff_Wisdom, Methodology) and is abstractable without losing personality. Design principles for the abstraction: **keep the conclusions, strip the citations** ("swab is a floor indicator, not a calibration metric" stays; "as seen in FW106 R11-12" goes); **preserve the opinions unapologetically** — this isn't a neutral framework, it's a strongly opinionated companion spec and the specificity is the product; **don't sand down the personality in the process of genericizing**. The harder design challenge is the *first session* — a stranger arrives with no run history, Claude needs to onboard gracefully (ask about strain and rig, explain what it'll track and why, suggest the baseline curve, start building from run 1). The methodology documents give enough to be useful immediately. Separate from the open-source work; could be done independently as a `companion-spec` branch or separate repo. **Known cuts and degradations when shipping publicly:** (a) *Equipment scoping* — scope explicitly to programmable setpoint devices (electronic rigs with repeatable curves); torch rigs have four uncontrolled variables where this project has one, and run-to-run comparisons don't build anything meaningful without controlled input. Exception path: torch users with an IR thermometer logging peak and drop temp on every run — note it as a way in, not an expectation. This is a deliberate scoping decision, not a limitation to apologize for. (b) *Evidence-trail degradation* — every methodology position in HANDOFF_WISDOM has calibrated confidence backed by specific run citations; strip the citations and the public version ships assertions without receipts. Stranger gets the conclusion, not the earned weight behind it. The onboarding framing needs to acknowledge this: "these positions are starting priors, your runs will confirm or revise them" — not presented as settled truth. (c) *The day-1 gap is real and shouldn't be papered over* — the companion experience compounds with history; the first few sessions are genuinely thinner. Don't fake depth that isn't there yet. The self-excising onboarding idea (Claude writes a blank-slate HANDOFF_STATE and stripped CLAUDE.md at end of onboarding, then removes the onboarding section) is worth exploring as an honest acknowledgment of this rather than pretending day 1 is session 94.

- **Draw velocity and cap-pearl dynamics** — FW106 R19 introduced draw effort as an uncontrolled variable: harder draws pull more airflow → more convective cooling of pearl and insert → faster within-draw heat shed; harder draws also spin the pearls faster via the Cloud Vortex cap. The two effects partially offset (faster spin = more heat delivery per contact; more airflow = more cooling). The deeper thread: pearl thermal mass × insert circumference × spin velocity × draw velocity all interact to determine heat-per-draw delivered to material.
